BUFFAlO RD COURT
LOCATION: BUFFALO
RD,NAIVASHA, KENYA
Programme: collective housing
Task: 6 2-bedroom units + 1 3-bedroom unit
Brief: The Buffalo Road Housing project located along
Buffalo Road near Lake Naivasha aims to integrate a
private residential community harmoniously into its
natural surroundings, maintaining a balance between
secure micro-density and quiet living quality.
The architectural intent is based on creating a linear
living layout that meets both the spatial efficiency
required for multi-unit housing and the desires for privacy
and open-air connectivity. The project's spatial footprint
and material selection are designed to establish a clear
residential spine, organizing six 2-bedroom homes
alongside a primary 3-bedroom anchor unit.
Centralized pedestrian pathways, dedicated parking
nodes, and lush perimeter green buffers guide circulation
across the site. With this project, the goal was to develop a
refined gated scheme that fosters close-knit community
interaction while offering a calm retreat along the Lake
Naivasha corridor.

9021 TERRACE
LOCATION:MURRAM ESTATE,KENYATTA ROAD
This residential design project addresses the critical challenge of optimizing spatial, structural, and operational
efficiency on an exceptionally tight 25 x 50 ft urban plot. To balance built area with open-air amenities, the main
building footprint is restricted to 25 x 40 ft, deliberately setting back the structure to leave a dedicated 10 ft rear
yard for private family outdoor space. The f ront setback is engineered to provide a full two-car parking apron and
utility entrance without encroaching on public setback requirements or restricting vehicular access.
The ground floor layout prioritizes uninterrupted visual lines and spatial fluidness by combining the living, dining,
and kitchen (LDK) functions into an open-plan configuration. This strategy eliminates unnecessary internal
partition walls, maximizing daylight penetration and natural ventilation through the central core. The upper
floor plan organizes the sleeping quarters, headlined by a fully ensuite master bedroom designed with dedicated
storage and circulation zones to maintain an uncrowded atmosphere despite the small overall square footage.
To resolve severe interior floor space limitations within the individual bedrooms, the design relies heavily on
custom wall-mounted joinery. All major furniture pieces, including wardrobes, study desks, side tables, and
foldable bed systems, are suspended or mounted directly onto vertical wall surfaces. This approach keeps the
floor area clear, maximizes usable walkways, and allows individual rooms to serve flexible, dual-purpose roles
throughout the day.
Structurally, the project incorporates expanded polystyrene (EPS) panels for both the floor slab construction and
primary wall elements. Using EPS technology significantly reduces the dead load of the building, speeds up
structural erection times, provides superior thermal and acoustic insulation performance, and optimizes cost
efficiency compared to traditional masonry systems. Technical outcomes for this project focus on microresidential spatial planning, custom kinetic and space-saving furniture detailing, EPS structural slab integration,
and high-density urban plot optimization

CHEVRON HOTEL
LOCATION:MTWAPA, KILIFI
The Urban Infill Hotel was a projrct that addressed the extreme spatial, environmental, and daylighting challenges of
highrise residential design. Submitted for the Inspirelli Architectural competition, the theme centered around unbuilt
designs, where participants were to submit self derived brief & self-selected sites. My approach restricted the design to an
1/8-acre site & the project a high-rise hotel in a affluent highrise neighborhood within the coastal area of Mtwapa,Kilifi
county.
Sandwiched tightly between two existing multi-storey structures with zero side window access, the design squeezes two
distinct room typologies, conference facilities, a public restaurant, a swimming pool, and structured basement parking into
a dense vertical envelope. The project turns a severely constrained, landlocked parcel into an architectural feature by
adopting an inward-focused orientation and radical problem-solving techniques.
The defining architectural feature of the primary front elevation is a vertical stack of alternating chevron (V-shaped)
balconies across four bay sections. The facade alternates floor-by-floor between timber louvered privacy screens and fullglass balustrades, giving the building a dynamic geometric identity while balancing solar shading and visual privacy.
Because adjacent structures block all side views, the hotel turns completely inward toward a deep central atrium light void.
Guest rooms open up to this central shaft, which is anchored at the ground floor by a raised swimming pool acting as an
artificial visual focal point.

 Thika Greens

Set within the secure perimeter of Thika Greens Estate, this luxury residence is an exercise in classical proportions adapted for contemporary living. The two-story, five-bedroom villa occupies a full acre, allowing the architecture to establish a commanding presence while maintaining an organic connection to its surrounding grounds.

The exterior facade relies on symmetrical order, polished rusticated plasterwork, and grand multi-story columns that frame the central entrance portico. Deeply set windows, decorative cornices, and refined balustrades pay homage to neoclassical tradition, elevated by high-performance glazing and integrated low-voltage accent lighting that emphasizes the building’s silhouette at night.

Inside, the ground floor opens into a double-height grand foyer with a sweeping curved staircase, immediately establishing the residence's scale. The layout separates formal entertaining venues from private family spaces, featuring an expansive formal living salon, a secondary family lounge, a chef’s kitchen with custom cabinetry, and a dedicated en-suite guest wing.

The surrounding acre is developed as an extension of the interior living space. Formal parterre gardens, manicured lawn terraces, and outdoor pavilion seating surround a perimeter infinity pool, creating clear axial vistas from every major living room across the landscaped grounds.

 Kitengela hearth

Situated in the arid landscape of Kitengela, this three-bedroom bungalow reimagines traditional neoclassical motifs through a grounded, climate-conscious palette. Designed as a single-story residence, the house balances classic symmetry with a low-slung, horizontal profile that anchors it into the savanna environment.

The exterior replaces crisp white neoclassical finishes with an earthy color palette featuring warm sand renders, textured local stone cladding, and terracotta roof tiles. Porticos and molded architraves are simplified into clean, linear geometries, retaining formal balance while avoiding excessive ornamentation.

The internal planning organizes around an open-plan central living and dining spine that opens onto a deeply shaded rear veranda. Large sliding doors and high-level clerestory windows encourage cross-ventilation, shielding the interior from afternoon heat while filling the space with diffused natural light and soft, warm tones.

The surrounding site design emphasizes native xeriscaping, combining drought-tolerant flora, gravel pathways, and natural stone paving. This low-maintenance landscape frame frames the home, maintaining a cohesive aesthetic connection between the architecture and its local context.

 Kerarapon villa

Located on the sloped terrain of Kerarapon, The Navy Residence is a two-story, five-bedroom modernist home defined by geometric form and an expressive interior palette. The architectural structure features sharp horizontal cantilevers, smooth concrete finishes, and expansive floor-to-ceiling glass assemblies that capture views of the surrounding green canopy.

The residence takes its name from its distinctive interior identity, where deep navy blue serves as the foundational anchor. Navy wall panels, bespoke millwork, and accent surfaces create an intimate atmosphere, balanced by rich timber ceilings, exposed stone feature walls, and matte black steel fixtures.

The spatial arrangement uses an open floor plan on the ground level, connecting the main living area, dining zone, and minimalist kitchen. Floating stair treads backed by glass guardrails lead to the upper level, where four primary bedroom suites—including a master suite with a private cantilevered balcony—overlook the double-height void below.

Large glass doors slide away to erase the boundary between the interior living space and an elevated timber deck. Warm exterior cedar cladding and perimeter planters integrate the house into its wooded site context, contrasting with the bold, dark tones of the internal design.
